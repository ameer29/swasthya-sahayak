## The problem
Millions of people in rural India and in low-income urban areas can't get reliable health guidance they understand. Most online health content is in English and full of jargon, so people rely on hearsay and forwarded messages, delay care, or take medicines incorrectly.

**Programme brief:** *How can voice- and text-based AI agents give non-English-speaking and underserved communities better access to reliable health information?*

## Who we designed for
| Persona | Context |
|---|---|
| **Asha, 62**, rural Uttar Pradesh | Basic phone, Hindi and a local dialect, weak internet |
| **Rajan, 34**, informal worker, Mumbai | A clinic visit costs him a day's wages |
| **Raju, 42**, farmer, Rajasthan | 2G data, low literacy |
| **Lakshmi, 28**, migrant worker and mother | Tamil; WhatsApp is her only internet |

**Key insight (Jobs-to-be-Done):** people don't want a diagnosis. They want clear next steps, in their own language, in a tool they already use.

## The solution
- **WhatsApp first:** no new app to install.
- **Voice first:** a large speak button for people with low literacy. Six languages: Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu and English.
- **Guided flow:** tap-to-answer questions (symptom, then duration) instead of a blank text box.
- **Always a human path:** home care, a clinic visit, or one tap to reach an ASHA health worker or doctor.
- **A provider dashboard** where doctors and health workers review escalated cases.

## My contribution
**1. AI design (Generative + Agentic AI)**

\`\`\`
Understand  →  Classify intent  →  Assess risk  →  Retrieve & simplify  →  Escalate to a human
(Indic STT)    (symptom/medicine)   (red flags)     (verified sources, GenAI)  (ASHA / doctor)
\`\`\`
- **Will:** translate and simplify verified information, guide the next step, send medicine reminders, route people to humans.
- **Will never:** diagnose, prescribe, replace a clinician, or keep more personal data than it needs.

**2. Success metrics**, designed around trust and safe outcomes rather than engagement:
- *Adoption:* activation, 30-day repeat use, share of sessions in local languages and by voice
- *Trust & safety:* accuracy in clinician spot checks (target above 90%), correct escalation rate, per-session helpfulness rating, NPS
- *Outcomes:* share of users who follow the recommended step within 2–3 days
- *Efficiency:* cost per conversation compared with a human helpline

**3. The WhatsApp channel idea** and **the executive summary**; I also presented the closing section.

## Process
Jobs-to-be-Done · Value Proposition Canvas · SWOT · ERRC · Blue Ocean · SCAMPER · Lean Canvas · MoSCoW · RICE → sketches → mid- and high-fidelity wireframes → two clickable prototypes. Market context comes from public sources, including India's Ministry of Health & Family Welfare and the WHO.

## What I'd do next
1. Run 5–8 interviews with real users, reached through ASHA workers, before building further.
2. Replace the script with retrieval-grounded generation, and test escalation against red-flag scenarios.
3. Treat **missed escalations** as the key failure metric.
